# SORA-SF-Clinical-Pathway

1. Skin Failure occurs when the skin and underlying tissue atrophy/die due to
“hypoperfusion”, or inadequate blood flow during critical illness, chronic disease
progression, or the dying process. Unlike pressure injuries caused by external
mechanical forces, skin failure results from “internal vascular compromise”
affecting tissue perfusion. Skin failure may often develop over “bony prominences”,
but the primary etiology refers to a systemic cause of ischemia rather than localized
ischemia due to mechanical forces as seen in pressure injuries. This spectrum of
skin changes may include:
a. Acute Skin Failure (ASF): critical illness-related 
b. Chronic Skin Failure: Progressive decline
c. End-Stage Skin Failure / Skin changes at the end of life (SCALE): natural skin
changes towards the end of the dying process (Kennedy Terminal Ulcer / KTU, TB-TTI,)

3. This tool serves to guide clinicians in determining a differential for wound etiology,
and provides a systematic approach to identify, differentiate, document, and
manage skin manifestations from other etiologies utilizing evidenced-based criteria
adapted from Hill/Peterson, Latimer, PAWSIC, and clinical practice. It does not
take the place of a provider, or final determination of legality/liability.

4. Use this tool when new skin/tissue impairment is identified in a patient with known or suspected critical illness, multi-organ dysfunction, chronic disease with acute decompensation, or clinical decline suggesting end of life — particularly when wound characteristics are atypical for classic pressure injury.

5. Recommendation: Full reassessment using SORA-SF recommended every 2 weeks, or sooner if clinical status changes significantly (new pressor requirement, code event, GOC change, transfer of care), and as needed.
